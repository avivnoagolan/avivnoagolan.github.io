Futura vs. Mr Eaves Modern

History of each typeface

Futura

Designer: Paul Renner, a German typographer and book designer based in Munich.

When & where: Renner developed Futura between 1924 and 1926, and it was released commercially in 1927 by the Bauer Type Foundry in Frankfurt, Germany.

Purpose: Futura was designed as a contribution to the New Frankfurt housing and urban-planning project, and it's built from geometric shapes, especially the circle, in a spirit similar to the Bauhaus design style of the period. Renner grounded the letterforms in circles, squares, and triangles, aiming for a sense of modernity suited to the machine age.

Mr Eaves Modern

Designer: Zuzana Licko, a Slovak-born American type designer and co-founder of Emigre Fonts, a digital type foundry based in Berkeley, California.

When: Mr Eaves (both the Sans and Modern versions) was designed in 2009.

Purpose and origin: Mr Eaves is the sans-serif companion to Mrs Eaves, Licko's much earlier (1996) revival of John Baskerville's classic 18th-century serif. Rather than simply stripping the serifs off Mrs Eaves, something Licko specifically wanted to avoid, she treated Mr Eaves as its own design that matches Mrs Eaves in weight, color, and overall structure while introducing distinct characteristics of its own. The Sans version stays closer to Mrs Eaves' humanist roots, while the Modern version pushes further from those roots toward simpler, more geometric shapes, with squared-off terminals and symmetric counters, giving designers two different degrees of contrast to pair with the serif original.

Overall Comparison

Futura and Mr Eaves Modern OT both use round, simple shapes and clean, squared-off edges. Futura is built purely from strict geometric forms, like perfect circles and triangles, giving it very even, mechanical strokes. Mr Eaves Modern keeps a bit of natural stroke variation and softer counters, which makes it easier to read in long blocks of text, while Futura works best in bigger, bolder display type.
